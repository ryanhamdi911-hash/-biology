Tier 1 — Mechanism
A drug blocks competitive inhibitors at enzyme active sites. Predict what happens to reaction rate if substrate concentration is tripled. Explain the mechanism fully.
Aim for 4 mark-worthy points
Key points: competitive inhibitor competes for active site → blocked by drug → active site now fully available → tripling substrate = more enzyme-substrate complexes forming per unit time → reaction rate increases significantly. Use: "probability of active site occupancy", "enzyme-substrate complex formation rate."
Tier 1 — Mechanism
Explain how a single base substitution mutation can have zero effect on the protein produced. Under what conditions would the same mutation type be lethal?
Aim for 5 mark-worthy points
Silent mutation → degenerate genetic code (multiple codons = same amino acid) → no amino acid change → protein tertiary structure unchanged → function preserved. Lethal if: active site residue changes → enzyme non-functional, OR stop codon introduced → truncated non-functional protein, OR mutation in regulatory region.
Tier 2 — Exam Pressure (6 marks)
Describe and explain how the body responds to a sudden drop in blood glucose concentration. Include the roles of specific hormones and organs. (6 marks)
6 marks — requires named structures and hormones
Low blood glucose detected by α-cells in islets of Langerhans (pancreas) → glucagon secreted into blood → liver cells (hepatocytes) stimulated → glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose) + gluconeogenesis → glucose released into blood → blood glucose rises → glucagon secretion reduces (negative feedback). Also: adrenaline (adrenal medulla) → same liver response + inhibits insulin release.
Tier 2 — Exam Pressure (5 marks)
A species of bacteria exposed to an antibiotic: after 20 generations, 95% of the population survives. Using natural selection, explain this outcome. Warning: Do NOT say bacteria "adapted" or "became resistant."
5 marks — mechanism only, no Lamarckian language
Random mutations in original population → some individuals had alleles conferring resistance by chance → antibiotic = selection pressure → non-resistant individuals die / fail to reproduce → resistant individuals survive and reproduce → pass resistance alleles to offspring → frequency of resistance alleles increases in population over generations → after 20 generations, 95% carry resistance alleles.
Tier 3 — Stretch & Integrate
A patient has a mutation preventing mRNA from being released from the nucleus. Predict THREE downstream consequences at the cellular and organism level. Justify each.
High difficulty — requires multi-system thinking
1. No protein synthesis → ribosomes in cytoplasm have no template → no polypeptides produced → loss of all cellular functions requiring proteins. 2. Enzyme deficiency → metabolic pathways fail → accumulation of substrates / absence of products → cell death or dysfunction. 3. Organism level: severe developmental failure → if embryonic, lethal; if somatic, organ-specific dysfunction (e.g. liver fails to produce clotting factors → uncontrolled bleeding). Bonus: immune system collapse, hormone deficiency, structural protein absence.
Tier 3 — First Principles
Why can't humans digest cellulose but can digest starch, even though both are made entirely of glucose? What does this reveal about the relationship between molecular structure and biological function?
Conceptual mastery question
Starch: α-1,4-glycosidic bonds → coiled/helical structure → amylase active site complementary to this shape → hydrolysis possible. Cellulose: β-1,4-glycosidic bonds → straight chains, hydrogen bonding between chains → rigid structural molecule → human amylase active site NOT complementary → cannot bind → no digestion. Principle: enzyme specificity (lock-and-key) = tertiary structure of active site determines function. Isomers with same atoms but different bond angles = completely different biological behaviour.

2-Minute Bulletproof Notes — The 6 Pillars
C
Cell → Function
Structure dictates role. Always ask why a cell is shaped/equipped that way. Form follows function — always.
E
Enzymes
Specific, reusable, affected by temp/pH/inhibitors. Active site = lock, substrate = key. Denatured ≠ deactivated — shape is permanently lost.
D
Central Dogma
DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → Protein. Mutations here cascade. Understand at which step each disease strikes.
H
Homeostasis
Negative feedback: Receptor → Coordination Centre → Effector. Always self-correcting. Know the specific pathway for blood glucose, temperature, and CO₂ levels.
N
Natural Selection
Variation → Pressure → Differential survival/reproduction → Allele frequency shift. Organisms never "choose" to adapt. Never use Lamarckian language.
A
Energy & ATP
ATP is the universal energy currency. Respiration (all cells) ≠ breathing. Photosynthesis stores energy; respiration releases it. Both are cellular processes.

Question
What is the difference between competitive and non-competitive enzyme inhibition?
tap to reveal
Answer
Competitive: blocks active site, reversible with more substrate. Non-competitive: binds allosteric site → changes enzyme shape → cannot be outcompeted by substrate.
Question
Why does temperature beyond the optimum decrease enzyme activity permanently?
tap to reveal
Answer
Heat breaks hydrogen bonds holding tertiary structure → active site shape changes → substrate can no longer bind → enzyme denatured (permanent, not temporary).
Question
What is the role of mRNA in protein synthesis?
tap to reveal
Answer
Carries genetic code from nucleus DNA to ribosome in cytoplasm. Sequence of codons (triplets) determines amino acid sequence during translation.
Question
How does natural selection change allele frequency in a population?
tap to reveal
Answer
Individuals with favourable alleles survive longer → reproduce more → pass those alleles to offspring → frequency of favourable alleles increases over generations.
Question
What triggers insulin secretion and what is its precise effect?
tap to reveal
Answer
High blood glucose detected by β-cells in islets of Langerhans → insulin released → liver and muscle cells increase glucose uptake and glycogenesis → blood glucose falls → negative feedback stops insulin release.
